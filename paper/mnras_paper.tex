\begin{abstract}
Dark-matter subhalos below the threshold of galaxy formation ($\lesssim10^8\,\msun$)
are a defining prediction of cold dark matter (CDM) and a discriminator against
warm (WDM), fuzzy (FDM), and self-interacting (SIDM) alternatives. Their flybys
imprint localized density gaps on thin Milky Way stellar streams. We present an
end-to-end, reproducible pipeline --- a physically faithful stream simulator, a
graph-neural-network (GNN) detector, impact-type characterization, a \emph{timeline
forward model}, and a look-elsewhere-corrected multi-stream significance framework
--- and report what it can and cannot measure. Our central methodological finding
is that \emph{simulator fidelity, not model architecture, sets the science reach}: a
units error that rendered simulated subhalos point-like (and a clumpy bespoke
generator) capped a GINEConv detector at AUC $0.62$ and made the network
out-of-distribution on real data. Replacing the bespoke generator with galpy's
validated distribution functions (\code{streamdf}/\code{streamgapdf};
\citealt{Bovy2014streamdf}; \citealt{SandersBovyErkal2016}) and fixing the
scale-radius bug raised the \emph{same} detector to test AUC $0.982$ with a zero
false-positive operating point, and rendered the real STREAMFINDER GD-1 catalog
\emph{in-distribution} (maximum input deviation $3.1\sigma$, versus $391\sigma$
before). We map the detector's completeness across impact type --- rising with
subhalo mass ($0.48\rightarrow0.72$ over $10^{7.5}$--$10^{8.5}\,\msun$) and falling
with impact age ($0.75\rightarrow0.39$ from $0.3$ to $1.4$~Gyr) --- and show that
single-gap characterization is degeneracy-limited: the detection embedding carries
almost no information about impact parameter ($R^2\approx0.02$) or epoch
($R^2\approx0.04$). Distinguishing DM models is consequently a \emph{population}
measurement; we quantify it as ${\approx}5$ detections for FDM ($10^{-22}$~eV),
${\approx}12$--$27$ for WDM ($3$--$6$~keV), and effectively unreachable for SIDM via
the mass spectrum. The timeline forward model recovers injected impacts exactly
(rank $0/36$) and, on real GD-1, cleanly detects the known gap ($\phi_1\approx50^\circ$)
but only marginally prefers a single-subhalo explanation. Across seven streams the
joint significance is null (Stouffer $Z=1.40$, $p=0.08$; Fisher $p=0.28$;
incoherent), a CDM-consistent upper limit. Naive per-stream significances up to
$19\sigma$ collapse to ${\approx}\pm2\sigma$ under the look-elsewhere correction.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Plain-language summary}
The Milky Way is thought to be swarming with thousands of invisible clumps of dark
matter. The lightest ones contain no stars at all, so the only way to find them is
by the gravitational ``wake'' they leave when they pass through something visible.
Thin streams of stars --- the shredded remains of old star clusters --- are the most
sensitive detectors we have: a passing dark clump carves a small \emph{gap} in the
otherwise smooth ribbon of stars. We built an automated system to find these gaps
and infer what made them. The single most important lesson is that the search only
works if the practice data are physically correct: our first simulator had a units
bug that made every simulated dark clump a million times too small, so the
AI detector trained on the wrong physics and performed no better than chance. After
the fix (using community-standard, peer-reviewed code), the same detector went from
chance (AUC $0.62$) to near-perfect (AUC $0.98$) and recognized the \emph{real} GD-1
stream as familiar rather than anomalous. The honest scientific bottom line: we can
reliably detect the gaps left by massive, recent impacts; inferring the exact mass
and age of a single dark clump from one gap is fundamentally ambiguous; and telling
apart \emph{kinds} of dark matter requires a population of detections (roughly
$5$--$30$ for the most favorable models). Applied to the seven streams available
today, we find no convincing detection --- consistent with standard cold dark
matter, reported as an upper limit.

\section{Introduction}
The abundance of low-mass dark-matter subhalos is among the sharpest predictions
separating cold dark matter (CDM) from warm (WDM), fuzzy (FDM), and self-interacting
(SIDM) alternatives. Below the threshold of galaxy formation ($\lesssim10^8\,\msun$)
these halos hold no stars and are detectable only gravitationally. Thin,
dynamically cold stellar streams --- tidal debris of disrupted globular clusters ---
are exquisite probes: a subhalo flyby imprints a density gap and a kinematic ripple
whose morphology encodes the perturber's mass and geometry
\citep{Carlberg2012,ErkalBelokurov2015}. GD-1's gap-and-spur has been read as
evidence for a dark perturber \citep{Bonaca2019}, and population analyses have begun
to constrain the subhalo mass function \citep{Banik2021}.

Turning a gap into a measurement is hard for two reasons. \emph{Degeneracy:} an
impact must be separated from baryonic perturbers, epicyclic density variations, and
selection systematics, and a single gap underdetermines the perturber's mass,
impact parameter, and epoch. \emph{Cost:} the forward model is expensive, making
likelihood-based search over many hypotheses slow. We address both with a graph
neural network (GNN) detector --- whose edge-conditioned message passing
\citep{Hu2020gine} respects the permutation symmetry of a member-star set and the
locality of kinematic perturbations --- and a constrained forward-model search we
call the \emph{timeline forward model}, which recasts detection as a physically
explicit rewind/re-impact/re-evolve comparison.

A central finding of this work, developed throughout, is that \emph{the dominant
control on what such a pipeline can measure is the fidelity of the training
simulator, not the sophistication of the network}. We document a concrete instance
(a scale-radius units bug that made every simulated subhalo point-like), show that
fixing it converts a chance-level detector into a strong one, and then report
honestly what the corrected pipeline can and cannot do.

\begin{plainterms}
Dark-matter clumps too small to hold stars can only be found by their gravity. When
one flies past a stream of stars it leaves a gap. We teach an AI to spot those gaps
--- but an AI is only as good as the practice problems it studies, and getting those
physically correct turned out to be the whole challenge.
\end{plainterms}

\section{The pipeline at a glance}
The system has five independently testable stages: (1) a \emph{simulator}
(\S\ref{sec:sim}) generating smooth streams and streams with a known subhalo gap
from validated galpy distribution functions, gated by a pre-flight validation
harness; (2) a \emph{detector} (\S\ref{sec:det}), a GINEConv GNN classifying a
stream as impacted or smooth, calibrated and out-of-distribution (OOD) aware; (3)
\emph{completeness and characterization} (\S\ref{sec:compl}--\ref{sec:char}),
mapping which impact types are detectable and inferring the perturber's properties;
(4) the \emph{timeline forward model} (\S\ref{sec:fwd}); and (5) \emph{population
significance} (\S\ref{sec:pop}) combining streams with a look-elsewhere correction
and coherence gate.

\section{The simulator, and why fidelity is everything}
\label{sec:sim}
\subsection{Generation with validated distribution functions}
Streams are integrated in an \code{MWPotential2014}-class Galactic potential
\citep[galpy, C \code{dop853} integrator;][]{Bovy2015galpy}. Progenitor initial
conditions are taken directly from the galstreams \citep{Mateu2023galstreams} 6-D
track at the centre of each observed $\phi_1$ window, reproducing literature proper
motions by construction. Smooth (``no-impact'') streams are drawn from the
action-angle distribution function \code{streamdf} \citep{Bovy2014streamdf}; streams
with a single subhalo gap from \code{streamgapdf} \citep{SandersBovyErkal2016}, a
subclass of \code{streamdf} sharing the identical smooth track and differing
\emph{only} by the encoded impact. This shared-track design is deliberate: the only
systematic difference between the two training classes is the gap itself, so the
detector cannot exploit a generator artifact. A particle-spray generator,
\code{streamspraydf} \citep{Fardal2015}, is used for cross-checks.

\subsection{Two simulator defects and their correction}
\label{sec:bugs}
The previous bespoke generator failed in two ways that together limited the entire
pipeline. First, a \emph{scale-radius units error}: the routine computed the
critical density in $\msun\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ but used it as
$\msun\,\mathrm{kpc}^{-3}$ --- a $10^9\times$ density error returning subhalo scale
radii ${\sim}10^3$ too small. Every simulated impact (including all earlier
\code{v3} results) therefore used an effectively \emph{point-like} subhalo
(${\sim}0.3$~pc for a $10^8\,\msun$ halo instead of ${\sim}0.25$~kpc), producing
unphysically sharp, spatially narrow velocity kicks. Second, a \emph{clumpy bespoke
spray} whose intrinsic density fluctuations (excess over the Poisson floor
${\approx}0.4$) swamped real gaps. Both are removed by adopting the validated
distribution functions and the corrected NFW scaling ($10^8\,\msun\rightarrow
r_s\approx0.25$~kpc). The consequence is large (Fig.~\ref{fig:simfix}): the
impact-vs-smooth separability rises from AUC $0.57$ to $0.94$, and a smooth stream
sits at the Poisson floor (gap-depth excess $0.01$).

\subsection{A pre-flight validation harness}
Every batch is gated by a validation harness: smoothness (gap-depth \emph{excess
over the Poisson floor}, $<0.15$), length versus the galstreams track, and
impact-vs-smooth separability (AUC $>0.85$) are \emph{critical} gates. The corrected
generator passes all (excess $0.01$, length $0.75\times$, separability $0.94$). Four
streams support the action-angle model cleanly (GD-1, ATLAS, Jhelum, Orphan); Pal~5
and Fj\"orm violate the isochrone action-angle approximation (near-circular orbits)
and are excluded by an allow-list.

\begin{plainterms}
Our practice problems were broken: a typo shrank every simulated dark clump to a
dot, so the gaps it made looked nothing like real ones. Switching to
community-standard code and fixing the typo is why everything downstream worked. We
also added an automatic ``smell test'' that refuses to run unless the practice data
look like real streams.
\end{plainterms}

\section{The detector}
\label{sec:det}
\subsection{Architecture}
Each stream's member stars form a $k$-nearest-neighbour graph ($k=8$) in normalized
$(\phi_1,\phi_2,\mu_1,\mu_2)$ space, ${\leq}1200$ stars per graph. Edge features
encode the local kinematic contrast a flyby perturbs; a GINEConv encoder
\citep{Hu2020gine} (${\sim}2.3$M parameters) with an auxiliary density-profile
branch produces a graph embedding and a binary impact/no-impact head. Training uses
AdamW, cosine warm restarts, mixed precision, and a $70/15/15$ split (seed 42) on
$15\,830$ simulations ($7\,830$ impact / $8\,000$ smooth) across the four supported
streams. The detection target is \emph{detectable} impacts (realized gap-depth
$>0.5$).

\subsection{Performance}
On the held-out test set the detector reaches AUC $0.982$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:roc}), best
validation accuracy $0.951$, with a zero false-positive rate at the $0.5$ operating
threshold. Temperature calibration ($T=0.54$) leaves the ranking unchanged. This
reverses the earlier \code{v3} result (AUC $0.62$) and is attributable entirely to
the simulator correction (\S\ref{sec:bugs}), not to architecture or hyperparameters.

\subsection{Application to the real GD-1 catalog}
Applied to the clean external STREAMFINDER GD-1 membership catalog
\citep{Ibata2021} (811 members), the detector flags an impact (\(p_\mathrm{impact}\)
$0.77$--$0.99$ depending on cuts), and the model-free gap finder independently
locates the known Price-Whelan--Bonaca gap at $\phi_1\approx50^\circ$
\citep{PriceWhelanBonaca2018}. Decisively, the network's inputs are now
\emph{in-distribution}: the maximum standardized feature deviation is $3.1\sigma$
with $0\%$ of features clipped, versus $391\sigma$ for the bundled catalog and
$13\sigma$ even after a dedicated error-domain-randomized retrain in the previous
pipeline. The corrected, realistic-noise simulator reproduces the real catalog's
feature distribution natively.

\begin{figure}
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig3_detector_roc.png}
\caption{Detector ROC on faithful held-out simulations (AUC $0.987$ for this
checkpoint; $0.982$ after temperature calibration). The earlier point-like-subhalo
pipeline reached only $0.62$.}
\label{fig:roc}
\end{figure}

\begin{plainterms}
After the fix, the gap-finder is nearly perfect on practice data and treats the real
GD-1 stream as familiar rather than anomalous --- which is what lets us trust what it
says about real data.
\end{plainterms}

\section{What kinds of impacts can we detect? (completeness)}
\label{sec:compl}
Detection depends on the \emph{type} of impact. Joining the detector's verdicts on
the test set to each simulation's true parameters yields the completeness map of
Fig.~\ref{fig:compl}. Completeness rises with subhalo mass ($0.48$ at $10^{7.5}$ to
$0.72$ at $10^{8.5}\,\msun$), falls with time since impact ($0.75$ for $<0.5$~Gyr to
$0.39$ for $>1.1$~Gyr, as gaps phase-mix), and is flat in impact parameter over the
close-encounter range probed ($0$--$0.35$~kpc). Overall completeness is $0.57$ at
zero false positives, and detection is essentially a step function in realized gap
strength. The detector is, in effect, a calibrated density-gap detector: it sees
massive, recent impacts that carve deep gaps and misses weak, old impacts that
perturb only the kinematics --- pointing to proper-motion-based features as the
next frontier.

\begin{figure}
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig4_completeness.png}
\caption{Detection completeness across impact type, measured on the test set.
Heavier and more recent impacts are more detectable; weak/old impacts are missed.}
\label{fig:compl}
\end{figure}

\begin{plainterms}
We reliably catch big, recent hits that gouge a clear gap. Small or ancient hits
that only nudge the stars' motions slip through, so our catch rate depends on what
kind of impact it was --- and we measured exactly how.
\end{plainterms}

\section{Characterizing the impact, and the single-gap degeneracy}
\label{sec:char}
Detecting a gap is easier than reading off what made it. Probing the frozen
detection embedding for the perturber's physical parameters shows it encodes mass
only weakly ($R^2\approx0.18$) and impact parameter ($R^2\approx0.02$) and epoch
($R^2\approx0.04$) essentially not at all: a binary detector discards everything
except ``is there a gap.'' This reflects a genuine degeneracy, since mass, impact
parameter, flyby speed, and epoch trade off in shaping a single gap's depth and
width. A dedicated multi-task model (mass/epoch regression head) is used to extract
what \emph{is} recoverable --- chiefly mass and recency --- and the forward model of
\S\ref{sec:fwd} is the complementary, physically explicit route through the same
degeneracy.

\begin{plainterms}
Finding the dent is easier than figuring out the exact size and speed of what caused
it --- many different impacts can leave a similar-looking dent. Some properties
(roughly how heavy, how recently) can be estimated; others are essentially
unknowable from a single gap.
\end{plainterms}

\section{Telling dark-matter models apart is a population measurement}
\label{sec:dm}
WDM, FDM, and SIDM do not change how any single impact looks --- they change how
many subhalos exist at each mass (the subhalo mass function). DM-model
discrimination is therefore a population inference. Combining each model's mass
function with our \emph{measured} completeness$(M)$ gives the distribution of
detected-impact masses (Fig.~\ref{fig:dm}a) and the number of clean detections
needed to distinguish it from CDM at 95\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:dm}b): ${\approx}5$ for FDM
($10^{-22}$~eV) whose cutoff sits in our sensitive band, ${\approx}12$--$27$ for WDM
($3$--$6$~keV), ${\approx}135$ for FDM ($10^{-21}$~eV), and effectively never for
SIDM, whose subhalo counts match CDM (SIDM would instead require the distinct gap
\emph{shape} of cored, low-concentration halos).

\begin{figure}
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig5_dm_family.png}
\caption{\emph{(a)} Detected-impact mass distributions differ between DM models only
where the mass-function cutoff falls inside our sensitive band
($10^{7.5}$--$10^{8.7}\,\msun$). \emph{(b)} Number of clean detections needed to
distinguish each model from CDM.}
\label{fig:dm}
\end{figure}

\begin{plainterms}
Different dark-matter theories don't change what one impact looks like --- they
change how common small clumps are. So you need a census of impacts, not a single
gap: about five clean detections for the most favorable theories, hundreds for
others, and for SIDM the mass count alone can never do it.
\end{plainterms}

\section{The timeline forward model and real-data application}
\label{sec:fwd}
For a detected gap we (1) estimate the impact epoch, (2) rewind the stream to its
unperturbed state, (3) re-inject a grid of encounters (mass $\times$ epoch $\times$
$\phi_1$) with the \citet{ErkalBelokurov2015} Plummer impulse and the corrected
scale radius, (4) re-evolve to the present, and (5) score each hypothesis against the
data. Injecting a known impact ($10^9\,\msun$, $1.5$~Gyr, $\phi_1=20^\circ$) returns
the truth as the top-ranked candidate (rank $0/36$; $\Delta\log M=\Delta t=
\Delta\phi_1=0$), beating the no-impact null by $90\%$. On real GD-1 the forward
model cleanly detects the gap ($\phi_1\approx50^\circ$; GNN \(p_\mathrm{impact}=0.99\),
in-distribution at $2.7\sigma$) but only marginally prefers a single-subhalo
explanation ($3.2\%$ improvement over the smooth null, with the mass pinned at the
low edge): with corrected, softer subhalos producing shallower gaps, a single
realistic subhalo struggles to reproduce GD-1's observed gap depth.

\begin{plainterms}
We can ``replay'' a stream: rewind it, drop in a simulated clump, fast-forward, and
check the match. This perfectly recovers impacts we plant ourselves. On the real
GD-1 gap it confirms a gap is there but cannot pin it on one specific clump.
\end{plainterms}

\section{Population significance across streams}
\label{sec:pop}
We combine seven streams (GD-1 on the clean STREAMFINDER catalog) with Stouffer's
$Z$ and Fisher's method, gated by a coherence requirement and corrected for the grid
search by a best-of-grid look-elsewhere null. The correction is decisive
(Fig.~\ref{fig:multistream}): naive single-cell significances as large as $19\sigma$
(Sylgr) and $12\sigma$ (Pal~5) collapse to ${\approx}\pm2\sigma$. The per-stream
evidence is incoherent ($71\%$ positive, mixed sign), and the joint result is null
--- Stouffer $Z=1.40$ ($p=0.08$), Fisher $\chi^2=16.6$ ($p=0.28$) --- best read as a
CDM-consistent upper limit. The naive-to-corrected collapse is itself a cautionary
methods result: unaccounted-for trials manufacture many-sigma ``signals'' from
noise.

\begin{figure}
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig6_multistream.png}
\caption{No coherent detection. Naive per-stream significance (grey) collapses under
the look-elsewhere correction (blue); the joint significance is null.}
\label{fig:multistream}
\end{figure}

\begin{plainterms}
Across seven streams we find no convincing sign of dark-matter impacts --- consistent
with the standard theory. We also show a trap: if you don't account for how many
places you looked, pure noise can masquerade as a huge discovery; correcting for it
makes those false signals vanish.
\end{plainterms}

\section{Limitations and systematics}
(1) Single-gap degeneracy (\S\ref{sec:char}): mass/geometry/epoch are only partially
recoverable from one gap. (2) Density-only sensitivity (\S\ref{sec:compl}): weak/old
kinematics-only perturbations are missed; proper-motion features are the next step.
(3) Action-angle model validity: clean generation is limited to eccentric streams;
multi-impact streams require \code{streampepperdf} (unavailable in this galpy), so
single-vs-multiple classification is deferred (gap counting is available model-free).
(4) Real-data volume: DM-model discrimination needs many clean detections
(\S\ref{sec:dm}). (5) Forward-model resolution and the single-encounter, impulse
approximation.

\section{Conclusions}
We built and stress-tested a complete, reproducible pipeline for dark-matter
subhalo-impact searches in stellar streams. Its clearest lesson is methodological:
simulator fidelity is the binding constraint. A scale-radius units bug that made
simulated subhalos point-like --- not the network --- capped detection at chance;
fixing it (with validated distribution functions and a pre-flight validation
harness) raised the same detector to AUC $0.982$ and made the real GD-1 catalog
in-distribution. With a faithful simulator we (i) mapped detection completeness
across impact type, (ii) showed single-gap characterization is degeneracy-limited,
(iii) reframed DM-model discrimination as a population measurement and quantified its
sample-size cost, and (iv) validated a timeline forward model that recovers injected
impacts exactly. Applied to current data, the pipeline finds no coherent
multi-stream detection --- a CDM-consistent upper limit --- and demonstrates how
look-elsewhere effects inflate naive significance by an order of magnitude. The path
to a measurement is now concrete: kinematic (not just density) features, more clean
membership catalogs, and the population sample sizes of \S\ref{sec:dm}.

\section*{Methods}
\textbf{Potential \& integration.} galpy \code{MWPotential2014}; C \code{dop853}
integrator (verified active); $R_0=8.0$~kpc, $V_0=220$~\kms.
\textbf{Distribution functions.} \code{streamdf} \citep{Bovy2014streamdf} for the
smooth track; \code{streamgapdf} \citep{SandersBovyErkal2016} with \code{impactb},
\code{subhalovel}, \code{timpact}, \code{impact\_angle}, $GM$, and $r_s$. The
action-angle setup uses $b=\mathrm{estimateBIsochrone}(\mathrm{pot},R/R_0,z/R_0)$
(${\approx}0.61$ for GD-1) and \code{nTrackChunks}$=5$; the impact angle shares the
sign of the modeled arm. \textbf{Subhalo physics.} NFW scale radius
$r_s=r_{200}/c$ with the \citet{Ludlow2016} concentration--mass relation and
$\rho_\mathrm{crit,0}=277.5\,h^2\,\msun\,\mathrm{kpc}^{-3}$; the
\citet{ErkalBelokurov2015} Plummer impulse
$\Delta v=-(2GM/w)\,\mathbf{b}/(|\mathbf{b}|^2+r_s^2)$. \textbf{Detector.} GINEConv
encoder, 18 node / 5 edge features, hidden 256, 6 layers, embedding 128, profile
branch (48 bins); binary head (BCE) with auxiliary mass/epoch regression; AdamW
(lr $3\times10^{-4}$, wd $10^{-4}$); temperature calibration on validation.
\textbf{Noise model.} Gaia DR3-like per-star errors \citep[][scale $0.5$--$2\times$]
{GaiaDR3}; radial velocity dropped at inference to match clean catalogs. Training on
foreground-contaminated streams collapses separability ($0.96\rightarrow0.56$), so
the operating regime is clean membership catalogs. \textbf{Statistics.} Per-stream
null from no-impact realizations; best-of-grid look-elsewhere null; Stouffer/Fisher
combination with a coherence gate.
